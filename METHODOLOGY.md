# Quantitative Methodology & Limitations: Pay-as-Produced PPA Pricing Engine

This document provides a comprehensive mathematical and financial breakdown of the valuation methodology, generation models, price simulations, and risk metrics implemented in the **German Renewable PPA Pricing & Risk Tool**.

---

## 1. Contract Structure: Pay-as-Produced (PaP) PPA

A **Pay-as-Produced (PaP)** Power Purchase Agreement is a bilateral contract in which the buyer (off-taker) agrees to purchase 100% of the actual electricity generated by a specific renewable asset ($Q_t$) at a pre-agreed fixed strike price ($K$, in €/MWh).

### Settlement Cash Flow Equation
For each settlement interval $t$ (hourly):
$$\text{CashFlow}_t = Q_t \times K$$

In financial terms, this contract is equivalent to a long position in electricity volume combined with a swap against wholesale day-ahead spot market prices ($P_t$):
$$\text{Financial Payoff}_t = Q_t \times (K - P_t)$$

Because the volume $Q_t$ fluctuates with weather and coincides with peak renewable production, the buyer is exposed to three distinct risk dimensions:
1. **Volume Risk**: Inter-annual and seasonal fluctuations in total MWh generated.
2. **Shape / Profile Risk (Cannibalization)**: Renewable generation is heavily concentrated during low-price hours.
3. **Balancing / Imbalance Risk**: Differences between day-ahead production forecasts and real-time turbine output.

## 2. Physical Asset Generation Models

### A. Solar PV Physical Generation Model
Meteorological data from ERA5 (via Open-Meteo) provides Global Tilted Irradiance ($GTI$, W/m²), Direct Normal Irradiance ($DNI$), and ambient temperature ($T_{\text{amb}}$, °C).

1. **Cell Temperature Estimation**:
   $$T_{\text{cell}} = T_{\text{amb}} + GTI \times \left(\frac{\text{NOCT} - 20}{800}\right)$$
   *Default NOCT (Nominal Operating Cell Temperature): $45^\circ\text{C}$.*

2. **Thermal Derating**:
   $$\eta_{\text{temp}} = 1 + \gamma_{\text{Pmax}} \times (T_{\text{cell}} - 25)$$
   *Default temperature coefficient $\gamma_{\text{Pmax}} = -0.38\% / ^\circ\text{C}$ for silicon crystalline modules.*

3. **DC Power Generation**:
   $$P_{\text{dc}} = C_{\text{dc}} \times \left(\frac{GTI}{1000}\right) \times \eta_{\text{temp}}$$
   Where $C_{\text{dc}} = C_{\text{ac}} \times (\text{DC/AC Ratio})$ (default DC/AC ratio: $1.20$).

4. **Inverter AC Clipping & System Loss**:
   $$P_{\text{ac}} = \min(P_{\text{dc}}, C_{\text{ac}}) \times (1 - \lambda_{\text{system}}) \times (1 - \delta)^y$$
   Where $\lambda_{\text{system}} \approx 6.0\%$ represents electrical and soiling losses, $\delta = 0.5\%/\text{yr}$ is degradation, and $y$ is the operational year.

---

### B. Wind Turbine Aerodynamic Power Curve Model
1. **Hub Height Wind Shear Extrapolation (Hellmann's Power Law)**:
   $$v(h_{\text{hub}}) = v(h_{\text{ref}}) \times \left(\frac{h_{\text{hub}}}{h_{\text{ref}}}\right)^\alpha$$
   * $\alpha = 0.143$ for German onshore terrain; $\alpha = 0.10$ for North Sea offshore.
   * $h_{\text{ref}} = 100\text{m}$ (ERA5 wind reanalysis level); $h_{\text{hub}} = 120\text{m}$ to $140\text{m}$.

2. **Empirical Aerodynamic Power Curve**:
   $$P(v) = \begin{cases} 
   0 & v < v_{\text{in}} \\
   C_{\text{rated}} \times \left(\frac{v^3 - v_{\text{in}}^3}{v_{\text{rated}}^3 - v_{\text{in}}^3}\right) & v_{\text{in}} \le v < v_{\text{rated}} \\
   C_{\text{rated}} & v_{\text{rated}} \le v < v_{\text{cut}} \\
   0 & v \ge v_{\text{cut}} \quad (\text{storm protection})
   \end{cases}$$
   *Standard IEC Class II/III parameters: $v_{\text{in}} = 3.0\text{ m/s}$, $v_{\text{rated}} = 11.5\text{ m/s}$, $v_{\text{cut}} = 25.0\text{ m/s}$.*

3. **Net Turbine Output**:
   $$P_{\text{net}} = P(v) \times (1 - \lambda_{\text{wake}}) \times (1 - \delta_{\text{wind}})^y$$
   Where $\lambda_{\text{wake}} \approx 8.0\%$ incorporates park wake effects and electrical transmission losses.

## 3. Coupled Price Simulation & Cannibalization Dynamics

To accurately evaluate PPAs, price simulation cannot be independent of asset generation. When thousands of MW of solar or wind feed power into the German DE-LU bidding zone, wholesale spot prices collapse.

### Price Process Formulation
$$P_t = S(t) + X_t - \beta_{\text{cannibal}} \cdot \text{CF}_t$$

Where:
* $S(t)$: Calibrated deterministic diurnal and monthly price shape (capturing morning peak, solar midday dip, evening peak).
* $X_t$: Mean-reverting Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) stochastic process with Poisson jump diffusion:
  $$dX_t = -\theta X_t dt + \sigma dW_t + J_t dN_t$$
  * $\theta \approx 0.15$: Reversion speed per hour.
  * $\sigma$: Calibrated residual volatility from historical SMARD spot data.
  * $J_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_j, \sigma_j)$: Poisson jump distribution modeling price spikes and deep negative plunges.
* $\beta_{\text{cannibal}} \approx 18.0 \text{ €/MWh}$: Empirical sensitivity coefficient reducing prices during hours of 100% renewable capacity factor ($\text{CF}_t$).

## 4. Fair PPA Pricing Waterfall

The tool computes the **Fair Fixed Strike Price ($K^*$)** through an institutional pricing waterfall:

```
[ Expected Forward Baseload Price ]   (e.g., 65.00 €/MWh)
                │
                ▼
   [ - Cannibalization Haircut ]      (e.g., -28.50 €/MWh: Profile shape risk)
                │
                ▼
  [ = Expected Capture Price ]        (e.g., 36.50 €/MWh: Generation-weighted)
                │
                ▼
     [ - Balancing Costs ]            (e.g., -2.50 €/MWh: Day-ahead forecast error)
                │
                ▼
     [ - Off-taker Risk Buffer ]      (e.g., -1.50 €/MWh: Downside capital margin)
                │
                ▼
    [ + Floor Guarantee Premium ]     (e.g., +0.00 to +3.50 €/MWh if Floor is active)
                │
                ▼
  [ = FAIR FIXED PPA STRIKE (K*) ]    (e.g., 32.50 €/MWh)
```

### Mathematical Definitions
1. **Baseload Price**:
   $$\bar{P} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N P_t$$
2. **Generation-Weighted Capture Price**:
   $$P_{\text{capture}} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^N P_t \times Q_t}{\sum_{t=1}^N Q_t}$$
3. **Capture Factor / Marktwertfaktor (MWF)**:
   $$\text{MWF} = \frac{P_{\text{capture}}}{\bar{P}}$$
4. **Cannibalization / Profile Haircut**:
   $$\Delta_{\text{profile}} = \bar{P} - P_{\text{capture}}$$
5. **Fair Fixed PPA Price ($K^*$)**:
   $$K^* = \mathbb{E}[P_{\text{capture}}] - c_{\text{balancing}} - c_{\text{risk}} + V_{\text{floor}}$$

## 5. Quantitative Risk Metrics & Tail Distributions

* **P10 / P50 / P90 Volumetric Uncertainty**:
  * In renewable energy project finance:
    * **P50**: Median expected production (50% exceedance probability).
    * **P90**: Conservative downside production (90% exceedance probability; bank debt sizing standard).
    * **P10**: Optimistic upside production (10% exceedance probability).
* **Value at Risk (95% VaR)**:
  $$\text{VaR}_{95} = \text{Revenue}_{\text{P50}} - \text{Percentile}_{5}(\text{Revenue})$$
* **Conditional Value at Risk (95% CVaR / Expected Shortfall)**:
  $$\text{CVaR}_{95} = \text{Revenue}_{\text{P50}} - \mathbb{E}[\text{Revenue} \mid \text{Revenue} \le \text{Percentile}_{5}(\text{Revenue})]$$
* **German Regulatory Risk (EEG § 51)**:
  Under § 51 of the German Renewable Energy Sources Act (*Erneuerbare-Energien-Gesetz*), statutory feed-in support is suspended if spot prices remain negative for consecutive hours (historical threshold: 3 to 6 consecutive hours). The tool tracks both the expected negative hour count and the unhedged negative cash drain.

## 6. Key Modeling Assumptions & Limitations

1. **Bidding Zone Single Price**:
   The model evaluates prices based on the unified German-Luxembourg (**DE-LU**) bidding zone. It does not model internal German transmission bottlenecks (North-South grid congestion), meaning local redispatch curtailment ordered by Transmission System Operators (Tennet, 50Hertz, Amprion, TransnetBW) is excluded from the spot price.
2. **Long-Term Grid & Storage Evolution**:
   As Germany commissions high-voltage direct current (HVDC) corridors (*SuedLink*, *SuedOstLink*) and utility-scale battery storage (BESS), the diurnal "duck curve" may flatten over a 10-year horizon. Forward simulations assume stationary cannibalization coefficients.
3. **Cross-Commodity Fundamentals**:
   Wholesale power prices in Germany are influenced by TTF Natural Gas prices, European EU ETS CO₂ allowances, and coal-to-gas switching levels. While the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process calibrates to historical volatility, structural regime changes in commodity markets require shifting the expected baseload parameter.
